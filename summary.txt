Wednesday, November 11, 2026, was a day of wars winding down and talks heating up. President Trump declared the war with Iran "militarily effectively over" after meeting Qatar's prime minister, AP reported, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio told the New York Times that US economic pressure had worked well enough that "there's really no need for major combat operations any longer" — though Washington warned it would still strike if Iran crossed red lines. Iran, meanwhile, told Al Jazeera the Strait of Hormuz, shut for more than 250 days, would "never return" to its pre-war status, and sent seven conditions for peace through Qatar. In Abu Dhabi, two days of US-run talks between Ukraine and Russia ended with no ceasefire but a deal on a large prisoner exchange — the biggest of the war, US envoy Jared Kushner said, NPR reported — while NATO scrambled jets after drones strayed into Baltic airspace. In Gaza, the truce frayed as Israeli strikes killed at least eight people, including a father and his young son, local health officials said. At the COP31 climate conference in Turkey, UN chief Antonio Guterres warned leaders that the slow response to climate change had put the world "on a path to destruction." In Washington, House Republicans blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved onto a military base amid Iranian threats, and a US Army officer was arrested in Britain on suspicion of spying for Russia, the BBC reported. China's trade surplus passed $1 trillion in the first ten months of the year, a record pace, the New York Times reported. And in Chile's Atacama Desert, one of the driest places on Earth, rare rains produced the biggest wildflower bloom in decades, NPR reported.
